Conditioning the female athlete
Keebra Park GRLA — what changes, and what does not
QRL Female Athlete
Physical Framework § 4
The principle The principles of training do not change for female athletes. The way they are applied must. Do not assume a conditioning program built for male athletes transfers across.
Five differences that change the session
What is differentWhat you do about it
Higher heart rate at the same relative work Smaller heart and stroke volume means she beats faster for the same output. Do not judge her off a male HR benchmark.
Better at sustaining moderate work More Type I fibres and about a third greater mitochondrial respiration. Repeat-effort work is a strength — use it.
Steeper power drop between maximal efforts At maximal and supramaximal intensity she may fade faster between reps. Give slightly longer recovery, not shorter.
Luteal phase raises core temperature 0.3–0.5°C Heat tolerance drops in the two weeks after ovulation. Move hard sessions to the cool part of the day.
Lactate threshold at a higher % of VO2max She holds a higher percentage before she tips over. HR-based zones built for males will under-prescribe her.
What the game asks for women's rugby league, measured
MeasureNumber
Relative distance, NRLW matchAbout 79–80 m per minute. State of Origin runs higher at ~81.6, Trans-Tasman Tests lower at ~73.4.
Peak 30-second intensityBacks ~189 m/min, forwards ~161 m/min. Over a 10-minute window both settle near 81–89.
A repeat high-intensity boutThree or more efforts — sprints, tackles, rucks — inside 21 seconds. Longest bouts run 49–64 seconds.
When bouts happenAbout 70% fall within five minutes of a try being scored. Condition for the critical periods, not the average.
Contact and running togetherRunning output drops as contact efforts rise. Training them separately trains neither.
The one rule that outranks the session plan
Technique rule: if running mechanics, landing or cutting start to fall apart, reduce the INTENSITY before you reduce the volume. Protective movement patterns fail earlier in female athletes than male at the same relative load (QRL Section 4). A missed bout costs one bout. A soft-tissue injury in week three costs the season.

Movement prep 3 drills
DrillDose Set-up, cue and why
Acceleration Wall Drill
Wall or partner
 3 x 10m Lean on a wall or partner, hips tall. Punch the ground away, shin angle out not down. Short, fast steps.
Teaches the accel shin angle before it is asked for at speed.
Build-Up Sprint
Cones
 3 x 20m 20m lane, build through the first 10m. Build, do not launch. Tall, relaxed jaw and hands.
Exposes top-end mechanics without a max effort cold.
Jog-to-Stick Decel
Cones
 4 x 10m Jog 10m into a cone, stop dead and hold 2 sec. Drop the hips, short braking steps, knee over the 2nd toe.
QRL 5.1: teach deceleration before acceleration. Decel is where the ACL goes.
Aerobic base 3 drills
DrillDose Set-up, cue and why
3-2-1 Continuous Game
Cones, whistle
 3 x 7 min, 2 min between 50m area. Coach calls every change: '3-2-1 sprint', '3-2-1 drop and jog'. On 'drop', controlled to the ground and back up fast, then straight into the next call.
QRL's own session. Coach controls the walk:jog:sprint ratio, so intensity is dialled live.
Ball-in-Hand Continuous
Balls, cones
 4 x 5 min, 90 sec between Grid 30x30. Continuous handling at stride, two-pass minimum before a turn. Hands early, eyes up. Skill does not drop because the lungs are working.
Gabbett 2006: skill-based games match traditional running for conditioning in rugby league.
Figure-8 Continuous
2 cones
 3 x 4 min, 60 sec between Two cones 20m apart, run a figure 8 around them continuously. Lean into the turn from the hip, not the ankle. Same pace each lap.
Low-impact aerobic volume with a constant gentle direction change.
Repeat effort 2 drills
DrillDose Set-up, cue and why
15:60 Repeat Sprint
Cones, timer
 8–12 reps 15 seconds maximal effort, 60 seconds active recovery (walk, do not stand). Full intent every rep. The recovery is a walk, not a rest.
QRL 4.1 prescribes exactly this: 10–30 sec efforts at 1:3 to 1:5.
10/20/30 Shuttle + Skill
Cones, ball
 6 efforts, 1:3 Sprint to 10m, back; 20m, back; 30m, back. Catch and pass at the line each time. The pass still has to be good on the last shuttle. That is the whole point.
Repeat effort with a skill under fatigue, which is how the game actually asks for it.
Game speed & RHIE 2 drills
DrillDose Set-up, cue and why
Tackle Tube Continuous
Tackle tube · contact
 4 x 1 min, 90 sec between Pairs. Run to a standing tube, tackle it to ground, carry it back. Repeat for the minute. Both arms wrap, get low, cheek to the right side. Technique first, speed second.
Tests the ability to keep making tackles once tired — the moment technique slips is the moment to stop.
Tackle Shuttle
Tackle bag · contact
 3 x 8 tackles Face down 3m from a bag. Get up, stay low, tackle, alternating lead shoulders. Alternate shoulders every rep. Chin off chest, eyes open, shoulder through.
Builds both shoulders. Female athletes are often one-sided in the tackle.
In-season 1 drill
DrillDose Set-up, cue and why
Short Sharp 6 x 20m
Cones, ball
 6 reps, 1:4 20m sprint, play-the-ball at the end, jog back. Sharp and clean. This is a top-up, not a flogging.
In-season volume comes down; intensity and quality stay up.
Game finishers 3 drills
DrillDose Set-up, cue and why
Off-Side Small-Sided Game
Balls, cones
 3 x 2 min 4v4 or 5v5, 30x30 grid, off-side rules. Support the carry. Nobody stands still.
Gabbett 2006: skill-based games are an effective alternative to traditional conditioning.
Two-Pass Transition Game
Balls, cones
 4 x 90 sec Minimum two passes before contact or touch. Turnover = instant transition. React to the turnover. The first three steps after it decide the set.
Trains the transition moment, where repeat-effort demand spikes.
Numbers-Up Attack v Defence
Balls, cones
 6 sets 4v3 or 5v4 attack. Swap after each set. Attack: hold the defender. Defence: slide and talk.
Decision-making under fatigue, which is the quality that fails first.
Contact drills follow QRL 5.3: neck and trunk bracing first, controlled positions demonstrated before load.
